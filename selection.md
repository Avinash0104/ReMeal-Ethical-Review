ReMeal privacy policy research notes



These notes document what ReMeal's public Privacy Notice says and identify questions for the group’s app walkthrough. The notice describes possible data practices. The permissions, settings, and behaviour of the app still need to be checked directly. \[1]



1\. Potentially excessive data collection (Sections 1A–1C)



&#x20;  The notice lists possible collection of location, device identifiers, and images used to verify identity. It also lists demographic details that people may volunteer in surveys. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

Customers should be able to tell which data ReMeal needs to process an order and which data it requests for other purposes.



2\. Unclear separation between service consent and advertising consent (Introduction and Sections 2F, 6, and 8)



&#x20;  The introduction treats providing personal data as agreement to the notice. The notice describes marketing opt-outs and options for limiting some tracking. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

Customers may find it difficult to make a separate, informed choice about personalised advertising when registering to buy food.



3\. Broad third-party sharing and profiling (Sections 2, 3C, and 6A)



&#x20;  The notice describes linking data across services and lists possible recipients, including analytics providers and credit-reporting organisations. It also describes possible matching with outside data. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern.

Customers may struggle to tell which information could go to each recipient and for what purpose.



4\. Unclear retention periods and deletion outcomes (Sections 4 and 8)



&#x20;  The notice describes retention by purpose without giving time periods for individual data categories. It describes deletion, anonymisation, and restrictions on access or use. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern. 

A customer requesting account deletion may have difficulty knowing what will be erased, what will remain, and for how long.



5\. Consent to policy changes through continued use (Section 9)



&#x20;  The notice treats continued use as acceptance of amendments, even when the user has not read them. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

Users may continue using the app without realising that the purposes or recipients of their data have changed.



6\. Unclear guardian consent for minors (Section 1F)



&#x20;  The notice asks guardians to prevent minors from submitting data and treats a guardian as consenting if a minor’s data is disclosed. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

The notice leaves open how a guardian learns of the disclosure and gives informed agreement.



7\. Handling of sensitive health information (Section 1D)



&#x20;  The notice describes possible processing of health information voluntarily shared with customer support, such as allergy information. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

A customer disclosing an allergy to resolve a food issue may be unsure how that information will be used or retained.



8\. Personal data about other people (Section 1E)



&#x20;  The notice relies on the submitting user to obtain the other person’s consent. \[1]



&#x20;  Ethical concern

The person whose details are provided may have limited visibility into how those details will be used.



The notice also describes marketing opt-outs, limits on employee access, and a process for making privacy requests. Record which safeguards are visible and how easy they are to use. \[1]



Ethics and literature references



\- ACM/IEEE-CS Software Engineering Code, clauses 3.12 and 3.13

Use 3.12 to assess whether the design and its documentation respect the privacy of affected people.

Use 3.13 to assess whether the purposes for collecting, sharing, and reusing data are properly authorised.

Clause 1.06 is relevant when comparing public privacy statements with app behaviour. \[2]

\- Contextual integrity.

Nissenbaum's framework asks whether a flow of information is appropriate for the situation in which it occurs. Applied here, it helps the group assess whether sharing data to fulfil a food order and sharing it for advertising fit users’ expectations in each context. \[3]

ReMeal selection research notes

\- What ReMeal does

ReMeal is a Malaysian mobile platform for buying discounted surplus food from businesses such as cafés, bakeries, and restaurants. The publicly available customer app gives the group a system it can examine directly. ReMeal website, Google Play listing

\- Why it fits the local or niche requirement

ReMeal serves a specific purpose within Malaysia's food-service sector by connecting customers with vendors selling surplus food. The Malaysian App Store displayed 33 ratings when checked for the selection section. Malaysian App Store listing

\- Why it is ethically relevant

Vendors can list individual items or Mystery Bags. Mystery Bags raise questions about what customers know about the food before paying, including ingredients and dietary suitability. ReMeal's privacy notice also describes possible collection of location, device, and transaction information. Seller guide, privacy notice
