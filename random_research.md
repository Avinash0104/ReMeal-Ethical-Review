# Additional Research for ReMeal Part 2

Supplementary research notes, rather than final report prose. Documentary evidence and findings from comparable platforms should be distinguished from first-hand evidence about ReMeal.

- **Refunds can affect vendors' future income.** ReMeal states that refunds issued after payout can create a negative store balance. Future sales must restore that balance before payouts resume. This raises an additional question about fairness to small vendors, particularly whether deductions are explained and disputed refunds can be appealed. The policy does not establish that unfair deductions have occurred. [Official refund guide](https://remeal.app/guides/refunds-and-payouts)

- **ReMeal controls refunds after order completion.** Sellers cannot independently process these refunds; only ReMeal's team can do so. Investigate whether this arrangement creates delays or limits either party's ability to resolve disputes. The policy establishes centralised control, but does not prove delays occur. [Official refund guide](https://remeal.app/guides/refunds-and-payouts)

- **Employee access restrictions provide positive ethical evidence.** ReMeal distinguishes between staff and administrative access: ordinary staff cannot see net order earnings, while a separate Finance role provides access to financial information. This supports analysis of confidentiality and limiting access according to job responsibilities. The published role descriptions do not independently verify that the restrictions are enforced correctly. [Official access information](https://remeal.app/guides/refunds-and-payouts)

- **New empirical research can supplement the study already used in Part 1.** Michelini et al. (2026) examined 1,077 Italian Generation Z respondents and found an association between food-sharing platform use and food waste. Use it to discuss household waste and rebound effects, while acknowledging that observational, self-reported findings from Italy do not establish ReMeal's impact.

  **Reference:** Michelini et al. (2026). *Reducing Food Waste Through Sharing Platforms: Unveiling the Rebound Effect*. *Business Strategy and the Environment, 35*(5), 6982-6997. https://doi.org/10.1002/bse.70501

- **Additional research on usable privacy notices.** Schaub et al. provide a framework for examining when, where, and how privacy information appears. This can support recommendations for short explanations at the point of data collection, rather than relying entirely on a lengthy policy. Apply the framework to the app's actual screens before making conclusions about ReMeal's notices.

  **Reference:** Schaub, F., Balebako, R., Durity, A. L., & Cranor, L. F. (2015). *A Design Space for Effective Privacy Notices*. In *Eleventh Symposium On Usable Privacy and Security (SOUPS 2015)* (pp. 1-17). USENIX Association. [Paper](https://www.usenix.org/conference/soups2015/proceedings/presentation/schaub)
