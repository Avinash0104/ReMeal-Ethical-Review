**Ethical Concerns**



1\. Buyers don’t know the contents, so they can’t check allergens, ingredients, or halal and dietary status.



2\. End-of-day surplus has unclear time since preparation, storage and handling. Delivery adds temperature and delay risk.



3\. Vendors decide what goes in the bag, so there is an incentive to include the least sellable items. Customers have no visibility or recourse.





**Shaping Behaviour**





1\. 2025 Berkeley Haas study found that surprise-bag apps may worsen waste, because retailers overproduce and consumers over purchase.



2\. In that model, customers who would have paid full price switch to discounted bags and then discard what they can’t use. This is cannibalisation plus rebound waste. 



3\. Commentary also notes that success is usually measured in weight of food moved, so true impact is hard to verify.

**Collecting Personal Data**



1\. ReMeal takes location, identifiers, contact info, purchases and usage data.



2\. Data is not encrypted.



3\. Location plus purchase history can reveal routines and, indirectly, income level and dietary or religious habits.





**Managing workers / targeting vulnerable users**



1\. Surprise-bag and discount platforms benefit retailers, but partners can become dependent on the platform’s pricing and commission. If ReMeal partners with local F\&B or bakeries, this is an ethical issue about fairness to suppliers.



2\. Small vendors, such as Ramadan bazaar hawkers in the partnership, may have little bargaining power.
